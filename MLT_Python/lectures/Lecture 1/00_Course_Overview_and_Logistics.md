## 1) Technical Explanation

The course **ES514: Machine Learning Techniques** is offered under the MS-I-AI/DS program with a credit structure of **4 (L-T-P: 3-0-2)**, meaning 3 hours of Lecture, 0 hours of Tutorial, and 2 hours of Practical (lab) per week.

**Administrative channels set up for the course:**
- **Google Classroom**: central hub for announcements, assignments, and materials.
- **Student Information Form**: a Google Form collecting Full Name, Email-ID, and WhatsApp Number for record-keeping and communication.
- **WhatsApp Group** (`MS-AI&DS-Batch 2026`): informal, fast communication channel for the batch.

**Evaluation Scheme (how your final grade is computed):**
| Component | Weightage |
|---|---|
| Minor Exam | 25% |
| Quizzes / Presentation / Class Participation | 15% |
| Major Exam | 40% |
| Lab Evaluation | 20% |

**Recommended textbooks:**
1. Aurélien Géron — *Hands-On Machine Learning with Scikit-Learn, Keras, and TensorFlow*
2. Kevin P. Murphy — *Machine Learning: A Probabilistic Perspective*
3. Andreas C. Müller & Sarah Guido — *Introduction to Machine Learning with Python*
4. Ian Goodfellow, Yoshua Bengio, Aaron Courville — *Deep Learning* (MIT Press)

## 2) Simple English Explanation

This is an introduction slide for a college course called "Machine Learning Techniques." It tells students:
- Where to find class updates (a website called Google Classroom).
- A form to fill in so the teacher has your name, email, and phone number.
- A WhatsApp group to join for quick messages.
- How your final marks will be calculated — some marks come from a mid-term exam, some from small quizzes/presentations, some from a final big exam, and some from lab work.
- A list of good books if you want to read more about the subject outside of class.

Think of it like the "welcome pack" you get on day one of any course — no machine learning content yet, just the ground rules of how the class will run.
