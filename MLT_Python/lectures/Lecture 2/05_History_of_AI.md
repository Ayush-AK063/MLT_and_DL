# Topic 5: A (Short) History of AI

Source: Lecture 2, slides 34–39. Enriched with web research (IBM, Dartmouth College, Stanford AI100 report).

---

## 1) Technical Explanation

The lecture presents AI's history as five broad eras:

**1) 1940–1950: Early Days**
- **1943 — McCulloch & Pitts**: proposed a Boolean circuit model of the brain — the first mathematical model showing that networks of simple "neuron-like" units could, in principle, compute logical functions. This is the conceptual ancestor of today's artificial neural networks.
- **1950 — Alan Turing's "Computing Machinery and Intelligence"**: introduced the famous question "Can machines think?" and proposed what's now called the **Turing Test** — a behavioral test where a machine is considered intelligent if a human judge cannot reliably distinguish its text responses from a human's.

**2) 1950–70: Excitement — "Look, Ma, no hands!"**
- Early AI programs emerged: Samuel's checkers-playing program (one of the first self-improving game-playing programs), Newell & Simon's Logic Theorist (proved mathematical theorems), Gelernter's Geometry Engine.
- **1956 — The Dartmouth Workshop**: widely regarded as the founding event of AI as a field; the term **"Artificial Intelligence"** was formally adopted here (proposed by McCarthy, Minsky, Rochester, and Shannon).
- **1965 — Robinson's resolution algorithm**: a complete algorithm for automated logical reasoning, foundational to symbolic AI/logic programming.

**3) 1970–90: Knowledge-Based Approaches**
- 1969–79: development of knowledge-based (rule-based) systems that encoded human expert knowledge directly as if-then rules.
- 1980–88: the **expert systems** industry boomed commercially (companies built AI systems that captured domain-expert knowledge for specific tasks like medical diagnosis).
- 1988–93: the expert systems industry collapsed — the first major **"AI Winter"**, a period of reduced funding and interest after expert systems failed to scale or generalize as promised.

**4) 1990–2012: Statistical Approaches**
- A resurgence of AI research grounded in **probability theory**, shifting focus toward explicitly modeling and reasoning under **uncertainty** (rather than rigid logic rules).
- General increase in technical/mathematical rigor across the field.
- Growth of intelligent agents and learning systems research — informally dubbed an "AI Spring."

**5) 2012–present: Excitement — "Look, Ma, no hands!" (again)**
- Driven by **Big Data**, massively increased **compute power** (especially GPUs), and modern **neural networks** (deep learning) — 2012 is widely cited as a turning point because of the AlexNet breakthrough in image recognition.
- Some re-unification of previously separate AI subfields (symbolic, statistical, and neural approaches increasingly combined).
- AI has moved from research labs into large-scale industrial deployment.

## 2) Simple English Explanation

AI's history can be told like a story with ups and downs — periods of huge excitement, followed by periods of disappointment, repeated a few times before arriving at today's AI boom.

- **1940s–50s (The Idea is Born)**: Scientists first proposed that simple computer circuits could behave a bit like brain cells (1943), and Alan Turing asked the now-famous question: "Can machines think?" (1950). He proposed a test — if you can't tell whether you're chatting with a human or a machine, the machine can be considered intelligent.
- **1950s–70s (Early Excitement)**: The field officially got its name — "Artificial Intelligence" — at a famous meeting at Dartmouth College in 1956. Researchers built early programs that could play checkers, prove math theorems, and solve geometry problems, and people were very optimistic.
- **1970s–90s (Teaching Computers What Experts Know)**: Researchers tried building "expert systems" — programs stuffed with rules from human experts (like doctors) to make decisions. This became a real industry in the 1980s, but by the late 80s it collapsed because these systems were too rigid and couldn't handle situations outside their exact rules. This crash is called the first "AI Winter" — funding and interest dried up.
- **1990s–2012 (Getting Smarter About Uncertainty)**: AI research became more mathematical, focusing on handling *uncertainty* using probability, rather than rigid rules. This laid the groundwork for modern machine learning.
- **2012–Today (The Big Boom)**: With huge amounts of data and much more powerful computers (especially graphics cards, GPUs), neural networks finally started working extremely well — leading to the explosion of AI we see today in image recognition, voice assistants, self-driving cars, and generative AI tools like ChatGPT.

## 3) Hinglish Explanation (Beginner-Friendly)

AI ki history ek roller-coaster jaisi hai — kabhi bahut excitement, kabhi bada disappointment ("AI Winter"), aur phir aaj ka bada AI boom.

**1) 1940–1950: Shuruaat (Idea paida hua)**
- **1943**: Scientists McCulloch aur Pitts ne dikhaya ki simple electronic circuits **brain cells (neurons)** jaisa behave kar sakte hai — yehi aage chal kar neural networks ka concept bana.
- **1950**: Alan Turing ne ek famous sawaal poocha — **"Kya machines soch sakti hai?"** Unhone ek test banaya (**Turing Test**): agar aap ye pata na laga sako ki saamne wala insaan hai ya machine, to machine ko "intelligent" maana jaayega.

**2) 1950–1970: Excitement ka daur ("Dekho Maa, bina haath ke!")**
- Early AI programs bane — checkers khelne wala program, geometry solve karne wala program.
- **1956**: Dartmouth College mein ek meeting hui jaha term **"Artificial Intelligence"** officially adopt kiya gaya — ye field ki "birthday" maani jaati hai.
- **1965**: Robinson ne ek complete algorithm banaya logical reasoning ke liye.

**3) 1970–1990: Knowledge-Based Approach (Expert ka gyaan machine mein daalna)**
- Researchers ne "Expert Systems" banaye — programs jinme doctors/experts ka gyaan **rules** ke roop mein daala gaya (jaise "agar symptom X ho to disease Y ho sakti hai").
- 1980s mein ye ek bada **industry** ban gaya (bahut companies expert systems bana rahi thi).
- Lekin **1988–1993** mein ye industry **crash ho gayi** — kyunki ye systems bahut rigid the, sirf exact situations mein kaam karte the, thoda alag case aane pe fail ho jaate the. Isko **"AI Winter"** kehte hai — funding aur interest dono kam ho gaye.

**4) 1990–2012: Statistical Approach (Probability ka istemaal)**
- Researchers ne rules chhod kar **probability aur uncertainty** (yani "shayad ye ho sakta hai" wala approach) pe focus kiya — ye zyada practical tha, kyunki real duniya mein sab kuch 100% certain nahi hota.
- Isse aage chal kar modern Machine Learning ki neev padi.

**5) 2012–Aaj tak: Bada Boom ("Phir se — Dekho Maa, bina haath ke!")**
- Bahut zyada **data (Big Data)** aur powerful computers (khaaskar GPUs) available hone lage.
- **Neural Networks (Deep Learning)** aakhirkar sahi tarike se kaam karne lage — 2012 mein AlexNet naam ke model ne image recognition mein bahut bada breakthrough diya.
- Ab AI sirf research labs mein nahi, balki **real industry mein** (Google, WhatsApp, self-driving cars, ChatGPT jaise tools mein) use ho raha hai.

**Yaad rakhne ka tarika (Timeline)**:
1940-50 (Idea) → 1950-70 (Excitement) → 1970-90 (Expert Systems + AI Winter) → 1990-2012 (Probability/Statistics) → 2012-Ab (Deep Learning Boom)
